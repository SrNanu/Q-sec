Models realistic quantum channel decoherence via Qiskit Aer's depolarizing_error ($\mathcal{E}(\rho) = (1-\epsilon)\rho + \frac{\epsilon}{3}(X\rho X + Y\rho Y + Z\rho Z)$). Rates above 11% cause the Secret Key Rate to drop to zero.

  1. Alice encodes bits: Maps classical bits to qubits using $\{|0\rangle, |1\rangle\}$ (rectilinear) or $\{|+\rangle, |-\rangle\}$ (diagonal).
  2. Quantum channel: Qubits transit through the optical fiber or free space, subject to depolarizing noise $\epsilon$.
  3. Eavesdropping (Eve): Any measurement without knowledge of Alice's basis collapses the state, causing irreversible errors.
  4. Bob measures: Projects each qubit onto an independently chosen random basis.
  5. Basis reconciliation (Sifting): Alice and Bob discard bits with discordant bases over a classical channel.
  6. Parameter estimation: QBER is estimated from a random sample. If $\text{QBER} < 11\%$, privacy amplification extracts the secret key.
  7. Asymptotic Secret Key Rate: $R \ge \max(0, 1 - 2h(\text{QBER}))$ under one-way classical error correction (Shor-Preskill / Devetak-Winter bound).
