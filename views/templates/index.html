Protocol

Four stages from random bits to a provably secret key.

Security rests on physics, not on computational hardness: measuring an unknown quantum state disturbs it, and that disturbance is measurable.

01 · ALICE

State preparation

Random bits $b_i$ are encoded in a random basis — rectilinear $\{|0\rangle,|1\rangle\}$ or diagonal $\{|+\rangle,|-\rangle\}$.

02 · CHANNEL

Quantum transmission

Photons cross a depolarizing channel $\mathcal{E}_\varepsilon(\rho)$ where an adversary may intercept and resend them.

03 · BOB

Measurement & sifting

Bob measures in his own random bases. Over a public channel both keep only positions where the bases agree (~50%).

04 · DISTILL

Key-rate estimation

A sacrificed sample estimates the QBER. Below 11%, a secret key survives at rate $R \ge 1 - 2h(\text{QBER})$.

Security bound

Where secrecy ends.

Every error Bob sees could have leaked to Eve. Error correction plus privacy amplification can remove that leakage — but only while the error rate stays under the Shor–Preskill threshold.

Asymptotic secret key rate
$$R = \max\bigl(0,\; 1 - 2\,h(Q)\bigr)$$
Binary Shannon entropy
$$h(p) = -p\log_2 p - (1-p)\log_2(1-p)$$
